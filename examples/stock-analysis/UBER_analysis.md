## 1. Executive summary

Uber has completed a genuine transformation from a cash-burning growth story into a profitable, free-cash-flow-generating platform business. In FY2025 it generated **$52.0B revenue (+18%), $193.5B gross bookings (+19%), $8.7B Adjusted EBITDA (+35%), and $9.8B free cash flow (+42%)**, on 13.6B trips and 202M monthly active platform consumers. Q2 2026 continued the pattern: **gross bookings +22% to $58B, Adjusted EBITDA +33% to $2.8B, and trailing-twelve-month FCF exceeding $10B** for the first time.

The investment case, however, is now dominated by a **valuation that has compressed sharply on autonomous-vehicle (AV) fears**, not by operating deterioration. The stock is ~$78.80, **down ~14% year-to-date in 2026 and ~22% below its September 2025 high of ~$102**, even as fundamentals hit records. The de-rating reflects three overlapping concerns: (1) the risk that Waymo/Tesla "own" the robotaxi end-state and cut Uber out of the value chain, (2) a visible slowdown in U.S. mobility and reported revenue growth (partly accounting-driven), and (3) two consecutive quarters of soft guidance.

My base-case view: Uber's core human-driver mobility + delivery franchise remains strong and cheap relative to its cash generation, but the market is correctly pricing in genuine AV/regulatory optionality risk. The company's "demand aggregator" AV strategy (20–30+ AV partnerships, a ~$10B+ capital commitment, lobbying for hybrid networks) is rational but unproven; the fraying Waymo relationship is a concrete warning sign. I see a wide range of fair value (~$70–$160) around a base case of roughly **$105–$120**, driven by continued ~25–30% EBITDA growth and a 16–17x multiple. This is informational analysis, not investment advice.

## 2. Company and industry overview

**What Uber is.** Uber operates a two-sided technology platform connecting consumers with independent drivers/couriers (Mobility and Delivery) and shippers with carriers (Freight). It operates in 70+ countries. As of Dec 31, 2025 it reports **three segments: Mobility, Delivery, and Freight** (per the FY2025 10-K). The platform's flywheel is cross-selling: consumers using both Mobility and Delivery generate roughly double the monthly trips of single-offering users, and ~61% of first-time Delivery consumers are new to the platform.

**Segment economics (FY2025, reported):**

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | Q4 2025 rev. growth | Q4 2025 Seg. Adj. EBITDA | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobility | ~$8.2B (Q4) | +19% YoY | $2.20B (+25%) | Ride-hailing; ~30% revenue margin |
| Delivery | ~$4.9B (Q4) | +30% YoY | $1.02B (+40%) | Uber Eats, grocery/retail, advertising |
| Freight | ~$1.27B (Q4) | flat | $0 (breakeven) | Logistics brokerage; small, cyclical |

Mobility is the profit engine; Delivery is the growth/engagement engine (now above a **$100B annual gross-bookings run-rate**); Freight is a small, cyclical, roughly breakeven business.

**Revenue model nuance (important).** Uber reports most of its revenue **net** (fees charged to drivers/merchants), but in some markets (e.g., UK) it is "principal" and reports **gross** (charging the end-user and paying the driver as cost of revenue). A **UK business-model change that began in 2026** moved driver-payment costs from cost of revenue to contra-revenue, which **mechanically reduced reported Mobility revenue growth by ~8 percentage points in Q2 2026** (Mobility revenue +1% reported vs. underlying growth) with **no impact on gross bookings, Adjusted EBITDA, or cash flow**. Any reading of Uber's top line in 2026 must adjust for this.

**Industry.** Uber competes in large, fragmented, low-switching-cost markets. U.S. rideshare: Uber ~72–76% share vs. Lyft ~24–28%. U.S. food delivery: DoorDash is the leader (~60–67%) with Uber Eats #2 (~23–30%). Internationally, Uber Eats holds leading positions in several markets (UK, France). Key competitive dynamics: price-sensitive consumers, multi-apping drivers, and now the emergence of robotaxis.

## 4. Earnings quality and balance sheet

**GAAP net income is noisy — treat with care.** Two distortions dominate:

1. **Equity-investment revaluations.** Uber holds large stakes in other companies (e.g., Didi, Grab, Aurora, and various AV partners). Q4 2025 GAAP net income of $296M included a **$1.6B pre-tax *headwind*** from these revaluations; Q2 2026 GAAP net income of $2.4B included a **$1.6B pre-tax *tailwind***. Management itself flags that GAAP net income "may continue to see swings."
2. **One-time tax items.** Q4 2024 net income of $6.9B included a **$6.4B tax valuation allowance release**. FY2024's $9.9B and FY2025's $10.1B GAAP net income both embed large non-cash benefits.

**The cleaner measures are Adjusted EBITDA ($10.0B trailing twelve months as of Q2 2026) and free cash flow ($10B+ TTM).** Even Adjusted EBITDA excludes real recurring costs — notably **stock-based compensation (~$1.7–1.9B guided for 2025)** and insurance — so FCF (which is burdened by both) is the most trustworthy earnings metric, and it is excellent.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026, per 10-Q):**

| Item | $B |
|---|---|
| Cash + short-term investments | ~5.4 |
| Restricted cash/investments | ~11.8 |
| Investments (marketable/equity stakes) | ~8.8 |
| Equity-method investments | ~3.8 |
| **Total debt (ST + LT)** | **~12.7** |
| Goodwill | ~9.5 |
| Total assets / total liabilities | 65.8 / 37.4 |

Uber is **net-debt-light on a simple basis (~$7B net debt after cash), and effectively net-financial-asset-rich if you include its investment portfolio**, which is offset by large insurance reserves and deferred tax assets. It retains an **accumulated deficit of $10.6B** (a legacy of years of losses) but is now consistently generating the cash to self-fund.

**Capital return.** Uber completed its inaugural **$7B buyback** and authorized an **additional $20B** (announced Aug 2025). It repurchased a record **$1.9B in Q4 2025**, reducing diluted share count ~2% YoY. This is a meaningful, growing support to per-share value and signals management's confidence in valuation.

## 5. Competitive position and management

**Moat assessment — moderate, with genuine threats.** Uber's advantages are real but not unassailable:

- **Network effects / liquidity** (more drivers → shorter wait times → more riders → more drivers) and a **two-product cross-sell moat** (Mobility + Delivery + Uber One membership, ~36M members) that Lyft and DoorDash can't fully replicate.
- **Brand and scale**: ~75% U.S. rideshare share, 70+ countries, unmatched marketplace data.
- **But** switching costs are low, consumers are price-sensitive (Gridwise data: 60%+ of riders reduced usage on price in 2025), and drivers multi-app. In U.S. food delivery, **DoorDash remains #1** (~2x Uber Eats' share).

**Management and capital allocation.** CEO Dara Khosrowshahi has presided over the margin/FCF inflection and is now betting heavily on AV. CFO transition is underway (Prashanth Mahendra-Rajah handing over to Balaji Krishnamurthy). Governance is standard dual-class-lite structure; insiders/founders retain influence. Capital allocation has improved materially (disciplined cost control, buybacks, and selective AV investments).

**The AV overhang is the crux.** The bull and bear cases diverge almost entirely here:

- **Uber's positioning:** be the "indispensable demand layer" — aggregate demand across 20–30+ AV partners (Waymo, Zoox, Rivian, Waabi, WeRide, Pony.ai, Baidu Apollo Go, Motional, Nuro, etc.), plus an **Uber–Nvidia partnership targeting 28 cities by 2028**, an internal **AV Labs** unit, and a stated **> $10B capital commitment** (equity stakes + charging infrastructure + vehicle offtake). Management argues AVs only unlock a larger market and that a hybrid human+robot fleet is more efficient (citing ~30% more trips/vehicle/day in Austin/Atlanta vs. robotaxi-only platforms).
- **The threat:** AVs are still **~0.1% of global rideshare trips** today, but **Waymo is the clear leader** (11 cities, 500K+ paid trips/week, 200M driverless miles, $16B raised) and **is pulling away from Uber** — Waymo plans to launch its own app in Austin/Atlanta in Jan 2028 (the Uber contract ends May 2028), and the Phoenix partnership already ended. Tesla (Austin launch, huge scale/resources) and Amazon's Zoox are pursuing standalone networks too. If AV leaders successfully run their own networks, Uber risks being disintermediated in its most valuable market.

**Most important sensitivities:** (1) the AV disintermediation vs. aggregation outcome (dominant driver), (2) sustained 20%+ gross-bookings growth vs. deceleration, (3) continued ~112% FCF conversion and buyback pace, and (4) driver-classification/regulatory rulings. Given the AV binary, the valuation range is genuinely wide; treat point estimates cautiously.

## 7. Catalysts and risks

**Upside catalysts**

- Continued 20%+ gross-bookings growth and EBITDA margin expansion (Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 both beat on bookings).
- Successful monetization of AV partnerships (Uber–Nvidia, Waabi, Rivian, etc.) and favorable "hybrid network" regulation.
- Aggressive $20B buyback reducing share count.
- Freight turning durably profitable as the logistics cycle recovers.

**Downside risks**

- **AV disintermediation:** Waymo/Tesla building standalone networks (Waymo app launches in Austin/Atlanta Jan 2028; Phoenix already split).
- **Reported revenue slowdown** (partly the UK accounting change) and soft guidance — Q2 2026 revenue missed, and Q3 2026 bookings/EPS guidance trailed consensus.
- **Driver classification / labor:** NZ Supreme Court ruled drivers are **employees** (Nov 2025); Australia NSW payroll-tax ruling (~$80M+); ongoing California Prop 22 litigation. The U.S. independent-contractor model has largely held (via arbitration and settlements), but this remains a structural tail risk that could raise costs materially.
- **Regulatory/competition:** Uber–Waymo policy fights; EU/platform rules; price-sensitivity among consumers and drivers.

## 8. Thesis breakers and monitoring checklist

**What would invalidate the base case (bull → bear):**

1. Waymo (and/or Tesla) achieves standalone network leadership in Uber's largest markets and users adopt AV-only apps — i.e., Uber loses its demand-gatekeeper role.
2. Gross-bookings growth breaks below ~15% on a sustained basis.
3. A major jurisdiction reclassifies drivers as employees (broad, not one-off) or imposes payroll-tax-equivalent liabilities at scale.
4. FCF conversion falls below ~80% of Adjusted EBITDA for an extended period.

**Signposts to monitor:**

- AV trips as % of Uber rideshare trips (currently ~0.1% globally) and AV city-count on Uber's platform (management targets 15 cities by end-2026; "leading AV-ride provider by 2029").
- Quarterly gross-bookings growth (constant currency) and Adjusted EBITDA margin vs. the ~4.5–5.0% range.
- Waymo/Tesla expansion announcements and regulatory outcomes (D.C. AV bill and similar).
- Buyback execution pace; diluted share count trend.
- U.S. mobility demand recovery and the UK accounting-change comparison effect lapsing.

## 9. Data limitations and open questions

- **Market data is point-in-time** (21 Aug 2026 close) and will be stale immediately; intraday market cap/EV vary with the investment-portfolio valuation treatment across sources.
- **Full-year 2025 segment-level figures** were only partially captured; I used the cleanest available Q4 2025 segment data and total-company figures. Exact FY2025 Mobility/Delivery/Freight EBITDA splits should be confirmed against the 10-K.
- **GAAP net income/EPS are materially distorted** by equity revaluations and tax items; I rely primarily on Adjusted EBITDA, non-GAAP operating income, and FCF.
- **Forward figures and my scenario valuations are estimates**, not sourced consensus; the AV end-state is genuinely uncertain and the range is wide.
- **Open questions worth researching next:** (1) the economic terms and take-rate economics of Uber's AV partnerships (who captures the margin), (2) the durability of Uber Eats' international gains vs. DoorDash's U.S. dominance, and (3) the exact cash-tax trajectory as historical losses are exhausted (management guides ~10–15% cash tax rate near-term, rising later).
